The answer in three sentences

Yes, heat costs energy — but only where an inverter is hotter than its peers, and only above about 75 °C. Plastic Omnium inverter 1 (JGMAE65009) produces 9 % less than its cooler siblings at midday when it is ≥ 75 °C (n = 1,609 intervals) and inverter 4 (JGMAE6500G) 4 % less (n = 1,990); together 1.8–2.9 MWh over the summer so far, 1.1–1.8 % of the plant's energy. Everywhere else — SAG's three Huawei units at up to 76 °C, Quimica Coyoacan at 75 °C, Taigene — the hot inverters keep up with their peers: no measurable loss, even at 75 °C.

How it was measured (the three methods, as proposed)

1 · Cooler-peer counterfactual, per 5-minute interval. For every inverter at every minute: output per kWp DC versus the median of its plant siblings that are ≥ 5 °C cooler and running ≥ 15 % of rating, scaled by the inverter's own cool baseline — its usual ratio to the peers when below 65 °C, per hour of day (inverter 4 at Plastic Omnium is shaded in the morning; an all-day baseline hid its midday derating — the v216 nightly job has exactly that blind spot, fix listed below). Clipping intervals (≥ 97 % of AC rating) are excluded. Loss = expected − actual while ≥ 65 °C, reported as a range: net (negatives count — the honest central number) to derating-only (positives only — the upper bound).

2 · The inverter against itself at the same conditions. Cool (< 65 °C) vs hot (≥ 70 °C) intervals in the same bin of irradiance (100 W/m²) and module temperature (5 °C). Inconclusive on this data: the hot inverter appears to make 3–13 % more in the same bin. Our irradiance and module temperature are hourly modelled values (Open-Meteo), not site instruments, so the bins do not really hold sun and module heat constant — hot intervals are simply the clear midday peaks. This method needs a Class-A on-site sensor (AGS-901); dropped from the numbers.

3 · Empirical ratio-vs-temperature curve. Output relative to all running peers, normalised by the own cool baseline, binned by the inverter's internal temperature — the fleet's own derating curve.

Fleet-wide the ratio is flat at 100 % through 70–75 °C (n = 3,272) and drops to 97.4 % above 75 °C (n = 4,126) — and that 2.6 % is almost entirely Plastic Omnium inverters 1 and 4. A hot inverter is not, by itself, a losing inverter.

Plastic Omnium — the case

Inverters 1 and 4 fall away above 70 °C (93 % and 98 % of baseline at ≥ 75 °C over all hours; −9 % and −4 % at midday); inverters 2 and 3 rise above 100 % in the same bins — they are only that hot at the clearest noon, exactly when 1 and 4 derate, so relative to the plant they look better. Both hot units run 8–14 °C above their siblings on the same roof, same sun: a unit-specific cooling problem (54 % of Plastic Omnium's hot intervals are ≥ 5 °C above the peer median; at SAG it is 2 %).

The hottest day (28 Aug) shows the plateau the theory predicts: inverters 2 and 3 climb to 75 % of DC rating, inverters 1 and 4 flatten at ~68 % from 11:30 to 14:30 while their temperature sits at 80–82 °C, then rejoin the peer curve as they cool after 16:00. That is inverter thermal limiting, not module heat — module heat would pull all four down together.

What this means for the alerts and the O&M list

1. The v220 rule (CRITICAL only with a measured shortfall ≥ 10 % against cooler peers; heat alone = WARNING) is confirmed by the data — SAG at 76 °C is a warning, not an outage.
2. Plastic Omnium inverters 1 and 4 are an O&M ticket with a number attached: 8–14 °C hotter than identical siblings on the same roof, 9 % / 4 % output loss above 75 °C, ~3 MWh a summer. Check fans, filters, heat-sink dust, enclosure clearance and sun exposure of those two units first; the thermal card will show the before/after.
3. The nightly thermal job (`thermal_daily`) should take its cool baseline per hour of day, as this analysis does — otherwise a morning-shaded unit's derating is under-counted (inverter 4: 1,185 kWh derating-only here vs 517 kWh in the v216 30-day figure).
4. Not thermal, but the same data showed it: Taigene inverter 6 (JFM7DXN013) sits 31 % below its peers even when cool — the known FT=302 unit; Budenheim inverter 1 (JJM4D4P01C) 20–40 % below inverter 2 per kWp at all temperatures — a DC-side or register problem, consistent with yesterday's string warning; Hirschmann and Ryder inverters differ by 10–35 % per kWp at all temperatures — most likely the DC ratings in the register, worth checking against the as-built.
5. Method 2 (self vs self) is the method that would settle inverter vs module heat beyond doubt, and it needs an on-site irradiance/module sensor — the AGS-701 R1 Class-A point in practice.

Caveats

Peer comparison assumes the siblings share sun, orientation and shading; the per-hour baseline absorbs fixed differences (DC size, morning shade) but not a fault that starts inside the window. 8 days (18–25 Aug) are missing from the telemetry with temperature. Losses are valued at the PPA tariff without IVA; CAPEX plants are shown without money. Thresholds: cooler peer ≥ 5 °C, running ≥ 15 %, clipping excluded ≥ 97 % AC, derating interval < 97 % of expected. Reproducible: `tools/thermal_deep_analysis.py` in the repo on the exported CSVs.
